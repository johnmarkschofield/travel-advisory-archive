Do not travel

due to **crime** ,**wrongful detention** , **health** , **kidnapping** , **natural disasters** , **terrorism** , and **unrest**. 

Do not travel to Afghanistan for any reason.

  * We urge American citizens in Afghanistan to leave immediately. The U.S. Embassy in Kabul suspended operations in 2021. The U.S. government cannot provide routine or emergency consular services to U.S. citizens in Afghanistan.
  * The United States does not have diplomatic relations with the Taliban.
  * The Taliban actively surveil and monitor travelers and resident foreign nationals, including dual nationals.
  * U.S.-Afghan dual nationals and U.S. citizens linked to NGOs and the media face higher risks of surveillance. 
  * The Taliban’s so-called “General Directorate of Intelligence” (GDI) and the “Ministry for the Propagation of Virtue and Prevention of Vice” (PVPV) are responsible for enforcing directives and edicts issued by Taliban leaders.
  * U.S. citizens are targets of kidnapping and hostage-taking and may become targets for violence.
  * For help leaving the country, please email biographic details, contact information (email and phone number), and U.S.__ passport number to [AfghanistanACS@state.gov](mailto:AfghanistanACS@state.gov).



##### **There is a high risk of wrongful detention of U.S. nationals in Afghanistan.  **

  * All U.S. citizens, including tourists and U.S.-Afghan dual nationals, are targeted for detention.
  * Lawful Permanent Residents who previously supported the United States in Afghanistan may also be targeted. For example, anyone who worked as an interpreter could be targeted.
  * The Taliban have harassed and detained aid workers, employees of faith-based organizations, members of the press, and humanitarian workers.
  * Foreigners are often viewed with suspicion. Reasons for detention may be unclear or arbitrary. 
  * Even if you are registered with the appropriate authorities to conduct business, the risk of detention is high.  
  * Detention conditions are severe. The Taliban do not permit the United States to conduct welfare checks on U.S. citizens in detention, including by phone.
  * Detention can be long. Even if a case is determined to be a wrongful detention, there is no guarantee of release.
  * While in detention, U.S. citizens have limited or no access to medical attention. They may face physical abuse. 
  * Women are not permitted to travel alone in Afghanistan and may be subject to detention if found traveling without a male escort.



##### Crime, Kidnapping, and Terrorism Risks

Multiple terrorist groups are active in Afghanistan, including ISIS-Khorasan (ISIS-K), al-Qa'ida, and Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), among others. Visit the U.S. Department of State's [country reports on terrorism](https://www.state.gov/country-reports-on-terrorism/) to learn more.

U.S. citizens  are targets of kidnapping and hostage-taking. and may become targets for violence.  Widespread poverty, severe unemployment, and a massive humanitarian crisis drive theft and robbery, including for U.S.-Afghan dual nationals. Additionally, organized criminal networks smuggle weapons, counterfeit goods, human trafficking victims, and natural resources.

##### Unrest 

Unrest and open conflict are heavily concentrated along the border regions. Fighting is prevalent in the Badakhshan region of Afghanistan near the Afghanistan-Tajikistan border.

##### Health

Basic medical care is available in Kabul and other major Afghan cities, but medical facilities are limited, especially for women, and may not provide care comparable to U.S. standards. Outside major cities, hospitals are scarce and often provide only basic services; access to adequate routine, emergency, and specialized medical care may be limited or unavailable.

##### Natural Disasters

Afghanistan is a seismically active region, with frequent and devastating earthquakes.  

  * Earthquakes have caused mass deaths and critical damage to homes and infrastructure.



##### Aviation Safety

The U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has not assessed Afghanistan's Civil Aviation Authority for compliance with international standards for aviation safety oversight. FAA’s [website](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/iasa) includes more information on its assessment program.

The FAA has issued a Notice to Airmen ([NOTAM)](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/us_restrictions/Afghanistan/doc/KICZ-NOTAM-A0007-25-Kabul-Pointer.pdf) and a Special Federal Aviation Regulation ([SFAR](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/us_restrictions/Afghanistan/doc/KICZ-NOTAM-AFGHANISTAN-FLIGHT-PROHIBITION.pdf)) due to risks to civil aviation operating within or in the vicinity of Afghanistan. For more information, U.S. citizens should consult the [Federal Aviation Administration’s Prohibitions, Restrictions and Notices](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/us_restrictions).
