Exercise increased caution

in **Ecuador** due to risk of **crime** , **terrorism** , **unrest** , and **kidnapping**. Some areas have increased risk. Read the entire Travel Advisory. 

U.S. government employees working in Ecuador are not allowed to travel to these areas __ due to safety risks:

  * In Guayaquil, Nueva Prosperina and Pascuales districts, and all areas south of Portete de Tarqui Avenue.
  * Huaquillas and Arenillas, in El Oro province.
  * Quevedo, Quinsaloma, and Pueblo Viejo, in Los Rios province.
  * Duran, in Guayas province.
  * Esmeraldas City and all areas north of Esmeraldas City, in Esmeraldas province.
  * The area north of Esmeraldas City to the Colombian border and east to Carchi province.
  * The U.S. government has limited ability to offer emergency services to U.S. citizens in these areas due to the safety risks _._  



##### Crime and kidnapping.

Crime is a widespread problem in Ecuador. Violent crime connected to drug trafficking is common, including murder, assault, kidnapping, and armed robbery.

  * Areas with transnational criminal organizations have much higher rates of violent crime.
  * U.S. citizens and U.S. lawful permanent residents have been victims of kidnapping and extortion.



##### Terrorism

  * There is risk of terrorist violence, including terrorist attacks and other activity in Ecuador. 
  * Visit the [U.S. Department of State's country reports](https://www.state.gov/country-reports-on-terrorism/) on terrorism to learn more. 



##### Unrest

Demonstrations happen across the country. They are usually driven by political or economic reasons.

  * Demonstrators often block local roads and major highways without warning. 
  * Demonstrations can also disrupt transportation and other essential services.  



##### Local and U.S. government assistance

  * Most of Ecuador’s land is empty and remote, especially outside big towns and cities. 
  * Help from local authorities  the U.S. government may be very limited in remote areas, causing delays.
